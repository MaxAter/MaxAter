# Sources

Every tempo window, form, meter, section length, energy function, instrument, and pairing in `catalog.json` points at an id below. Patterns that could not be sourced were not encoded. This app never generates or suggests song key.

## Policy

- **S00** — Product rule: arrangement output excludes key / mode / scale suggestions.

## Form and section function

- **S01** — Jay Summach, “The Structure, Function, and Genesis of the Prechorus,” *Music Theory Online* 17.3 (2011). Verse–chorus dominance; prechorus share rising to ~60% of verse–chorus songs by the late 1980s. https://www.mtosmt.org/issues/mto.11.17.3/mto.11.17.3.summach.html
- **S02** — Jeffrey S. Ensign, *Form in Popular Song, 1990–2009*, Ph.D. diss., University of North Texas (2015). 402 Billboard year-end Top 20 songs; verse–chorus, prechorus, postchorus, and bridge families. https://digital.library.unt.edu/ark:/67531/metadc822808/
- **S03** — Hit Songs Deconstructed, *2025 #1 Hit Spotlight* highlights PDF. 2025 #1 length 3:29; pop 44% of #1s; pre-chorus in 78%; post-chorus in 56%; first chorus after 1:00 in 33%; Luther 69 BPM, form I–A–PC–B–A–PC–B–C–O; instruments (synth bass, electronic drums, acoustic guitar, electric piano, strings); synth prominence 94%→44% (2021–2025); blended acoustic/electronic drums 0% of 2025 #1s. https://www.hitsongsdeconstructed.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Hit-Songs-Deconstructed-2025-1-Hit-Report.pdf
- **S04** — Hit Songs Deconstructed, *Pop Trends* Q1 2011 excerpt. Most popular top-10 structure A–B–A–B–C–B; high bridge share; pre-chorus more common on #1s than on #2–#10; short intros; solos generally out; many false endings. https://reports.hitsongsdeconstructed.com/wp-content/uploads/2011/06/HSD_2011_POP_TRENDS_EXERPT_Q1.pdf
- **S05** — Ashton Yount Stroud, “Codetta and Anthem Postchorus Types in Top-40 Pop from 2010 to 2015,” *Music Theory Online* 28.2 (2022). Postchorus as a closing, hook-bearing section after the chorus. https://www.mtosmt.org/issues/mto.22.28.2/mto.22.28.2.stroud.html
- **S06** — Drew Nobile, “Teleology in Verse–Prechorus–Chorus Form, 1965–2020,” *Music Theory Online* 28.3 (2022), and the pop-drop / anti-telos discussion citing Sloan & Harding. https://mtosmt.org/issues/mto.22.28.3/mto.22.28.3.nobile.html
- **S11** — Billboard Pro / Hit Songs Deconstructed, “2024 No. 1 Hit Focus Report: Hot 100 Trends” (2025). Three choruses in 69% of 2024 #1s; average time to first chorus 45 seconds; average #1 length 3:30; 80s/retro influence notes. https://www.billboard.com/pro/hit-songs-deconstructed-2024-number-1-hit-focus-report-hot-100-trends/
- **S12** — Hit Songs Deconstructed, “The State of the Hot 100 Top 10: 2025 Year in Review.” Acoustic-sounding drums in 42% of top 10s (first time ahead of electronic since 2014); post-chorus hook center in 63% of 2025 top 10s. https://www.hitsongsdeconstructed.com/hsd_wire/the-state-of-the-hot-100-top-10-2025-year-in-review/
- **S13** — Hit Songs Deconstructed, *2024 #1 Hit Spotlight* highlights PDF. Country #1 instrument list (acoustic & synth bass, acoustic drums, acoustic & electric guitar, banjo, fiddle); 81 BPM example; form with vocal breaks; drum split 44% electronic / 38% acoustic / ~20% blend; synth bass 56% vs electric bass 38%; electric guitar 31%, acoustic guitar 25% and country-only in that #1 sample. https://www.hitsongsdeconstructed.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/The-Hit-Songs-Deconstructed-1-Hit-Focus-Report-Highlights.pdf
- **S14** — Hit Songs Deconstructed, *2025 Staying Power* highlights PDF. 53 BPM ballad/soul example; instruments: electric bass, acoustic drums, electric guitar, organ, snaps, non-bass synth; form I–A–B–A–B–IB–B. https://www.hitsongsdeconstructed.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/2025-Staying-Power-Highlights-from-the-Report.pdf
- **S30** — Hit Songs Deconstructed platform description: deconstructions cover structure, instrumentation, energy, vocal production, and hook categories on Hot 100 top 10s. https://www.hitsongsdeconstructed.com/the-hit-songs-deconstructed-platform/

## Tempo

- **S07** — Robin Frederick, “Write a song like ‘Don’t Start Now’” (2020). 124 BPM disco-tempo dance-pop; four-on-the-floor kick; funk bass; synth chords locked to the beat; cowbell. https://robinfrederick.com/hit-songwriting-dont-start-now-by-dua-lipa/
- **S08** — Wikipedia, “Don’t Start Now (Dua Lipa song),” citing Billboard and other contemporaneous reviews. Nu-disco; 4/4; 124 BPM; 3:03; funk bass; handclaps; crowd noise; cowbells; synth bursts; accented strings; piano chords; house beat; electro-violin stabs; laser SFX. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don't_Start_Now_(Dua_Lipa_song)
- **S09** — Gary Trust, “State of the Hot 100’s Top 10, Mid-Year 2022,” *Billboard*, reporting Hit Songs Deconstructed: average top-10 tempo 99 BPM; more than half over 100 BPM; some 170–179 BPM titles. https://www.billboard.com/pro/hot-100-mid-year-2022-hit-songs-deconstructed/
- **S10** — Laurence Horton, “Tidy Tuesday: Billboard Top 100” (Spotify metadata on Billboard titles, 1958–2021). Mean tempo 120 BPM; yearly means about 114–126. https://laurencehorton.github.io/billboard-top-100/
- **S19** — Joe Bennett, “Anatomy of the hit: Ariana Grande’s ‘7 rings’” (2019). Sparse reverbed-synth intro; 70 vs 140 BPM ambiguity; trap 32nd-note hats; soft-attack skipping bass; delayed drum entry. https://joebennett.net/2019/04/19/7rings/
- **S20** — Kathy Iandoli, “What Makes Trap … Trap?” *TIDAL Magazine*. Producer DAW tempos often 140–180 while the felt pulse is ~70–90; long-decay 808 as kick-and-bass; hat triplets/rolls/swing; God’s Plan–era pitched 808s. https://tidal.com/magazine/article/what-makes-trap-trap/1-72722
- **S21** — Samplified, “How to Make a Trap Beat.” Typical trap DAW 120–160 (often 140); performers often feel 60–80 (half-time). Used only for the double-time/half-time convention, not for invented drum recipes. https://samplified.us/blogs/news/how-to-make-a-trap-beat

## Meter

- **S23** — Michael Tauberg, “Anatomy of a Hit Song (2000–2018).” 221 Billboard #1s: 96% in 4/4. (Key statistics in that essay are not used.) https://michaeltauberg.medium.com/women-are-dominating-popular-music-43c5ed83534b
- **S24** — David Bennett piano, “97% of pop is in 4/4…” (listen of 933 Billboard Hot 100 year-top-40 unique titles since 2000): 96.57% pure 4/4; remainders mostly 6/8, 12/8, 3/4, or mixed — not 5/4 or 7/4 in that bestseller set. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AcAUcbwI8QE
- **S25** — Matt Bannister, Billboard #1 exploratory analysis: #1s are overwhelmingly 4/4; 3/4 is uncommon and historically older on the Hot 100. https://github.com/mspbannister/dand-p4-billboard/blob/master/Billboard_analysis__100417_.md

## Section length

- **S26** — Ensign 2015 (same as S02): module length usually a multiple of 4 bars — commonly 8, 12, or 16; as short as 4; seldom longer than 16.
- **S27** — Point Blank Music School, “How to Structure a Song Part 1.” Pop songs often ~80 bars in 8-bar (or multiple-of-8) sections; verses typically 8 or 16; choruses typically 8, sometimes doubled later; middle eight often 8. https://www.pointblankmusicschool.com/blog/how-to-structure-a-song-part-1/
- **S28** — Open Music Theory (Gotham et al.), “Introduction to Form in Popular Music.” Pop/rock sections typically 8–24 bars. https://human.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Music/Music_Theory/Open_Music_Theory_2e_(Gotham_et_al.)/07%3A_Popular_Music/7.04%3A_Introduction_to_Form_in_Popular_Music

## Energy, drop, production

- **S15** — Alyssa Barna / related University of Delaware thesis text, *Formal Function of the Pop-Drop in Popular Music*. Verse sparse; prechorus density gradient; mute before drop; Don’t Start Now prototype (4-bar intro, soft verse, added bass/perc, prechorus ascent, cut-out, drop). https://udspace.udel.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/20f2edcb-a633-4e85-b215-6650d1615e1c/content
- **S16** — Brad Osborn, “Formal Functions and Rotations in Top-40 EDM,” *Intégral* 36 (2023). Riser (pitch, low-cut, snare build, no four-on-the-floor); drop (kick returns); later drops with effected vocal samples; chorus vs drop as syntactic vs statistical climax. https://theory.esm.rochester.edu/integral/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Integral_Vol36_Osborn.pdf
- **S17** — Nobile 2022 (same as S06): verse low-energy initiation, prechorus buildup, chorus telos; riser–drop mapped onto prechorus–chorus; anti-telos chorus + drop.
- **S18** — *Sound on Sound*, “Inside Track: Dua Lipa ‘Don’t Start Now’” (Ian Kirkpatrick). Layered bass/sub; LFO Tool vocal ducking 10–40%; dotted-eighth delay into Saturn; formant + microshift vocal chain. https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-dua-lipa-dont-start-now
- **S22** — Hit Songs Deconstructed, “Bad Guy: Techniques Spotlight.” Synth-focused post-chorus; accompaniment-pull S.I.A.s; spoken title tag; trap-influenced bridge. https://reports.hitsongsdeconstructed.com/articles/bad-guy-techniques-spotlight/
- **S29** — Open Music Theory, same chapter as S28. Section boundaries via drum fill, sudden louder dynamic, more active/doubled guitar, higher vocal register, added background vocals; closing rhetoric (repetition, thinning, late intensification, fade).

## What was not encoded

- Chord progressions and keys (including I–V–vi–IV lists).
- Unsourced “typical BPM” tables from blogs that did not show a corpus.
- Invented instrument pairings (for example, arbitrary synth-preset recipes).
- 5/4, 7/4, or other rare meters that Bennett notes exist but did not appear in the 933-song bestseller listen.
- Claims that blended acoustic+electronic drums are the current #1 norm (HSD 2025 says 0% of #1s).
